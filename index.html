Platform

Quest's Virtual Display becomes a two-way bridge to the PC

A quiet day before Thanksgiving: no launches, funding or announcements from Apple, Google, Snap or Xreal in the window. The one substantive story is UploadVR's tally of what Meta's first-party PC-mirroring app (formerly Remote Desktop) has gained when paired with Microsoft's Mixed Reality Link on Windows 11. The headset microphone now works as a PC audio input. Windows can already use the passthrough cameras, or show you as your Meta Avatar in video calls. An update earlier this month added wired USB streaming with no QR code or PIN, and a new "Computer Inputs" setting lets the PC's mouse and keyboard leave the desktop window and control the headset.

Why it matters Meta is building the headset-as-monitor case ahead of its productivity-pitched VR Glasses, due spring 2027. The input forwarding is undocumented and still rolling out, and Touch controllers can make the cursor vanish. Quest 3 and 3S only.

Miru and Biel Crystal team up on dimmable lenses for AR glasses

Vancouver's Miru Smart Technologies and glass maker Biel Crystal will jointly develop electrochromic lenses, which change tint electronically, for AR glasses and transparent displays. They plan a production-grade pilot line for customer evaluation, which they describe as groundwork for volumes "in the millions".

Why it matters Dimming is what keeps a see-through display readable in sunlight, and a volume glass manufacturer is the missing half. No timeline, site or terms are given.

Geology · Deep sea

Explosive-eruption rock from 6,148 m down the Mariana Trench wall

Dives by the ROV Jason on the inner trench wall near the Challenger Deep brought back pyroclastic rock from 6,148 m, reported as the deepest such deposits documented in any ocean. They lie in the forearc, where the mantle is normally too cool to make young lava. The work comes from seven dives along roughly 300 km of wall on a late-2024 cruise, led by Robert Stern at the University of Texas at Dallas.

Why it matters Explosive volcanism should be suppressed at that pressure; the authors' working idea involves supercritical water. The vents have not been found, dating is under way, and nothing below the vehicle's 6,500 m limit was sampled. I read a Nature highlight and news coverage; the paper page would not open.

Climate

A loop of meltwater shields a Greenland fjord from ocean heat

Published today in Nature Geoscience: a recurring clockwise loop of glacial meltwater in West Greenland's Uummannaq trough cuts the fjord off from the warm Atlantic Water below, limiting the heat that reaches the glacier. The loop is driven by summer discharge and shaped by wind. The team, from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Woods Hole, combined a 2021 icebreaker survey with historical data, a reanalysis and a 1/60-degree model.

Why it matters Meltwater that slows further melting is a feedback ice-sheet models do not resolve at fjord scale. Abstract and figure captions only: it gives no numbers, and the survey is a snapshot.

Deep sea

A glass salp that never leaves the deep

MBARI reports a new genus and species, Vitreosalpa gemini, living between 280 and 2,200 m. The release calls it the first of about 50 known salp species found only in the deep sea. The description draws on more than 20 years of ROV sightings and collected specimens. It swims slowly, feeds and respires far less than surface salps, and carries twin embryos, which is rare in the group.

Why it matters Salps are fast carbon exporters at the surface; a slow deep relative shows the group's range, and what long video archives can yield. Release only. "Exclusively deep" rests on where it has been seen.

Ecology

Sociable, predictable dolphins live longer

Forty years of records on 457 wild Indo-Pacific bottlenose dolphins in Shark Bay, Western Australia, each followed for 2 to 35 years, show group size rising and then falling with age. Individuals that kept larger and steadier groups tended to live longer. The study, led from the University of Queensland, appeared in PLOS Biology on 24 November.

Why it matters Individual-level ageing data of this length are rare for wild marine mammals. It is correlational and from one population, group size stands in for sociability, and the summaries I read give no effect sizes.

Technology

Shifting fisheries could put more subsea cable under trawls

A National Oceanography Centre-led assessment overlays projected future fishing on today's cable routes. By 2100, about 34,600 km of existing cable could be newly exposed to bottom trawling, and trawling could intensify along a further 157,000 km. Small islands served by one or two cables are most at risk.

Why it matters Fishing gear is already a leading cause of cable faults. These are exposure lengths, not fault rates, and the write-up names no scenarios or uncertainty ranges. Read via Eco Magazine; the paper is in Climate Risk Management.

arXiv · Perception

Chameleon: obstacles that blend into the scene but still say "careful"

Yu, Jin and colleagues re-texture physical objects in VR so they fit the virtual scene while carrying visual cues about contact risk. In a reaching task with 42 participants, they compared this against scanned real appearances with red warnings and against uniform environment textures. Participants rated the risk-aware textures better on four aspects of cue clarity.

Why it matters It treats the real room as something to redesign, not just fence off. Abstract only: the outcomes are ratings, with no numbers, tests or venue given.

AR · ISS

For cyclists, any AR text slows reactions; the bottom of view is least bad

Presented at ISS tomorrow morning, though the paper appeared a year ago. He, Elvitigala and colleagues (Monash) had 20 people ride a 500 m campus path wearing a HoloLens 2, with short text notifications at the top, right or bottom of view. Reaction time to an audio cue rose from about 1.7 s with no notification to 2.4–2.5 s with one, wherever it sat. Bottom placement was rated safer and more noticeable than top, and 18 of 20 preferred it.

Why it matters A measured attention cost for glasses on the move, as consumer models ship. One traffic-free path, text only, and reaction times from 17 riders.

arXiv · Audio

Does spatial audio need elevation? Often not

SoundFleXR (Woodard, LaViola and colleagues) compares plain stereo, azimuth-only HRTF rendering and full HRTF on localisation tasks with head rotation only and with free movement. Azimuth-only was statistically non-inferior to full HRTF on total angular error, even at a 5-degree tolerance. Stereo matched full rendering only at coarser tolerances, 15 degrees but not 10.

Why it matters A principled case for cheaper audio pipelines where precision needs are modest. I saw the abstract through a mirror site because arXiv throttled requests; it gives no sample size.

arXiv · Cybersickness

Three sliders to report sickness, symptom by symptom, while in VR

Saha, Quarles and colleagues tested separate in-headset sliders for nausea, eye strain and disorientation against a single overall slider, with 52 participants between subjects on a VR rollercoaster. Each slider tracked its matching questionnaire subscale most closely, and three sliders cost nothing measurable in usability, workload or attention.

Why it matters Continuous, symptom-level labels are what sickness predictors lack. Abstract only, no coefficients, one stimulus.
