Walkabout Mini Golf stops making new courses for Pico

It is Thanksgiving and a quiet day: no hardware, funding or platform announcements in the window, so this section is mostly content news. The most telling item is in a DLC notice. Mighty Coconut says its next course, Valhalla Splash, arrives 3 December on Quest, PC VR, Galaxy XR and PS VR2, but not Pico. October's course was the last for that platform, because QA and release costs there outgrew the revenue. Existing purchases keep working, but Pico players cannot join friends on newer courses.

Why it matters Walkabout is about as cross-platform as VR games get, so it is a fair gauge of which stores pay their way. UploadVR sets it beside two rounds of layoffs, a DLC price rise and the end of the iOS version.

A fan mod puts the Ocarina of Time remake in first-person VR

HookshotVR is a fan-made fork of an open-source PC recompilation of the game, not anything from Nintendo. It adds a first-person view, tracked controllers, hands-on use of several items and a wrist display. It runs on OpenXR, is still labelled alpha at version 1.0.2, and requires players to supply their own copy of the game.

Why it matters A neat teaching case in retrofitting a third-person design for embodied play, of uncertain legal lifespan.

Marine science 5 items + briefs · 5 min

Deep sea · Geology

A drowned coral atoll on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge

A 35-day expedition on Schmidt Ocean Institute's Falkor (too), led by Jose Angel Perez of Brazil's Universidade do Vale do Itajaí, mapped and filmed the Romanche Fracture Zone, a 950 km transform fault in the equatorial Atlantic. The team reports a fossil coral atoll about 5 million years old and 300 m tall, its top 900 m below the surface, on a volcanic ridge rising 4,100 m from the seafloor. They also filmed a sponge and coral garden at 4,300 m and a field of zoanthids, anemone-like relatives of corals, the size of a football pitch at 1,200 m.

Why it matters An atoll at that depth records how far the ridge has subsided, and these are described as the first images of life along the fault. It is an expedition announcement, not a paper: new species are suspected, not confirmed, and the dating methods are not described.

Climate · Ecology

The world's hottest reefs have lost about half their functional range

Published today in Scientific Reports: sixteen years of coral surveys in the Persian/Arabian Gulf show that repeated marine heatwaves cut functional richness, the spread of ecological roles the corals fill, by about 50%. Communities now lean on fewer taxa and have shifted toward hardier but structurally simpler growth forms. The study is led by Samara Zinman at Nova Southeastern University.

Why it matters Gulf corals are often cited as the heat-tolerant survivors; this suggests survival comes with flatter, less varied reefs. Abstract only, from an early accepted version.

Carbon · Atmosphere

What the Baltic breathes out changes with the season

A University of Copenhagen-led team measured the sea-air exchange of 73 volatile organic compound signals continuously for 51 days at Utö island in summer and autumn 2024. Early sulfur emissions, peaking at 33.6 µmol of dimethyl sulfide per square metre per day in late July, fell away as cyanobacteria declined. Oxygen- and nitrogen-bearing compounds swung between uptake and wind-driven bursts of release.

Why it matters These gases seed aerosols and clouds, and most models treat the sea as a steady source. One fixed station; flux uncertainty runs from 20% to over 50% depending on the compound.

Antarctica · Geology

Seafloor troughs mark the routes warm water could take to an East Antarctic glacier

New depth soundings from the Japanese icebreaker Shirase, about 900,000 points collected over the 2016–2018 summers, map Lützow-Holm Bay. The Shirase Glacier connects to the deeper shelf through an entrance about 740 m deep, while neighbouring glacier fronts sit behind sills of roughly 480–580 m. The main trough lines up with a magnetic boundary, which the authors read as old bedrock structure steering glacial erosion.

Why it matters Where deep warm water can reach is set by sill depth. The grid is interpolated from single-beam soundings because the multibeam sonar was out of service, and the paper has no ocean measurements showing warm water arriving.

Biodiversity

DNA barcodes plus taxonomists find 40% more species on Flemish Cap

A Spanish team (IIM-CSIC, Vigo) identified 434 invertebrate specimens from a 2022 trawl survey on Flemish Cap, off Newfoundland, by both morphology and DNA barcoding. About 86% of sequences matched something in public databases, but only 33% gave the same answer across markers and repositories. Combining the two methods raised the species list from 88 to 123.

Why it matters A caution for eDNA monitoring of vulnerable habitats: reference libraries are still thin, especially for sponges and cnidarians. Abstract only.

arXiv · Security

MetaScanner: deceptive interfaces hidden inside social VR worlds

A thin week on arXiv, with only a handful of XR papers. Lee, Kim and colleagues describe four attacks in which scripts and interactive objects built into a VRChat world lead visitors into actions they did not intend. An ethics-approved study with 30 participants confirmed that the attacks work. Their defence scans a world's assets before anyone enters; it caught all four attacks in test worlds and took about 5 seconds on average across 38 real assets.

Why it matters Dark patterns become harder to spot when the interface is the world itself. Accepted at Computers & Security. Abstract only: no success rates, and detection was tested on the authors' own attacks.

arXiv 2611.25608
arXiv · AI and XR

Reading code in a headset: summaries help overview, cost effort

Zakharov, Sergeyuk and colleagues interviewed 13 experts, then tried two XR prototypes with 12 professional developers. In one, code already looked at fades out (4 people); in the other it is replaced by a short description of its purpose (8 people). Head direction triggers the offer and manual input confirms it. Descriptions helped overview and recall, but checking them against the source, headset limits and unfamiliar controls added work.

Why it matters An early, qualitative look at attention-aware programming tools in XR. Small uneven groups, no quantitative results; submitted to a workshop at IUI 2027.

arXiv 2611.24334
TVCG · Hand tracking

Hand pose estimation that copes with hands that differ

Zhou, Liang and colleagues, accepted at TVCG, build a benchmark that simulates missing parts of the hand's structure, and a model meant to stay accurate on such hands without losing accuracy on complete ones. Evaluation is on standard datasets, with no user study and no figures in the abstract.

Why it matters Headset hand tracking assumes five fingers; people with limb differences are routinely failed by it. The abstract does not mention XR, so that application is my reading.

arXiv 2611.29163
arXiv · Animation

Teaching a virtual sparring partner real martial arts

Wen, LiKamWa, Turaga and colleagues captured Tang Soo Do motion, trained a repertoire of skills with reinforcement learning, then tuned it for targeted strikes and evasion. A prototype runs on Quest headsets, and study participants rated the motion's plausibility, smoothness and form. The abstract gives no sample size or scores.
