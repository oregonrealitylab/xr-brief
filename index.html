Launch

Google and Samsung put Android XR glasses on sale

Pre-orders opened 12 November through Warby Parker (three styles at $395, US only) and Gentle Monster (six styles from $449, US and South Korea). Deliveries start 10 December. All styles weigh about 41 g, promise up to nine hours of battery, carry a 12 MP camera on Qualcomm's AR1 chip, and work with iPhones as well as Android. There is no display; Gemini is the interface.

Google leaned on privacy: bystander faces are redacted before Gemini sees an image, and recording stops if the indicator light is covered. Gentle Monster's first batch reportedly sold out within four days.

Why it matters The price and specs sit squarely on Ray-Ban Meta. The contest is now about assistants and frame brands, with consent-by-design as a stated feature.

Apple's glasses: reveal targeted for WWDC 2027

Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reported on 15 November that Apple plans to announce display-free glasses at WWDC 2027 and ship by the end of that year. The prototype runs a cut-down visionOS on a chip derived from Apple Watch silicon and uses a magnetic three-pin charger. Road to VR separately reports the hardware design is settled and Siri is the centre of the product.

Why it matters These are reports, not announcements, and the timing can slip. If accurate, all three major platform owners will have camera glasses within about a year.

Marine science 4 items + briefs · 5 min

Geology · Science

Underwater landslides recorded as they happen

Sonar arrays and a seafloor mooring at the Homathko Delta in Bute Inlet, British Columbia, picked up about 30 fully submerged landslides in 18 days, three of them in fine detail. The sandy seabed fails slowly, over minutes to hours, in a process called breaching, and then feeds flows that run kilometres down a 50 km channel. MBARI and Durham University led the work with ten institutions.

Why it matters Breaching may need different hazard planning for cables, pipelines and shorelines than sudden slope collapse, and it moves carbon and pollutants offshore routinely.

National Oceanography CentreEnglert et al., Science, 12 Nov
Carbon · Science

The ocean is taking a growing share of fossil carbon; land has stalled

Scripps researchers used more than 30 years of paired atmospheric oxygen and CO2 records to separate the two sinks. The ocean absorbed about 2.8 billion tonnes of carbon a year from 1990 to 2019, roughly 35% of fossil emissions, and that uptake has been rising. Net land uptake peaked around 2009 and has not kept pace.

Why it matters Most models expected land uptake to grow with CO2. An independent, measurement-based check says it has not, which puts more weight on an ocean sink the authors say may also slow.

Deep sea · Sci Reports

Seabed mining nodules are habitats on the inside too

A MARUM team in Bremen opened 244 polymetallic nodules from the Clarion-Clipperton Zone and counted 4,139 small animals living in the cracks, 17 per nodule on average and 245 in the busiest one. The communities differ from those in the surrounding mud and look alike across sites up to 1,000 km apart.

Why it matters Nodules grow a few millimetres per million years. Mining them for nickel, cobalt and copper would remove a habitat that cannot regrow on any human timescale.

Climate · NASA

This El Niño is tracking the 1997 giant

Sentinel-6 sea-level maps from 12 October closely match TOPEX/Poseidon's from October 1997, with warm water piled along the equator toward South America. NOAA's October outlook gave an 83% chance of strong to very strong conditions for January to March 2027. A repeat of 1997–98 would mean a wet, flood-prone winter for California and the southern US, though JPL scientists stress the comparison is a best guess.

Awards · ISMAR 2026

What the ISMAR best-paper list says about the field

Eight papers took best-paper awards in Bari last month. Grouped by theme, from titles and abstracts:

  • Guidance beyond vision A forearm vibrotactile band that adapts direction cues to arm posture for VR navigation, and a wayfinding study whose title states the result: actionable guidance beats map and compass cues.
  • Subtle input One Finger Warrior, a thumb-to-index technique for mobile use. This is the kind of input display-free and small-display glasses need.
  • Other senses Smell as a context cue to carry procedural skills from mixed-reality training to real tasks, and hand-outline visuals that share felt roughness between VR collaborators.
  • Wellbeing CyberSelf, embodied self-distancing for emotional support in VR.

Also recognised Honourable mentions include vergence-accommodation conflict in gaze-based 3D selection, how VR systems affect virtual navigation for blind people, and pinch versus point poses for drawing.

Thread · AI + XR

Language models are becoming part of the interaction loop

A Cambridge group's ISMAR paper handles open-ended gesture and voice commands in 3D by generating code at runtime instead of matching a fixed command set. ISMAR also hosted a first workshop on AI-mediated heads-up interaction for wearable mixed reality, framed around deciding when and how glasses should speak up.

Why it matters This is the research side of this week's product news. Glasses with no display leave timing, modality and interruption as the main design questions.
