Platform · Content

Somnium Space wants to be the front door for PC VR mods

It is still a holiday weekend, so today has one real announcement and a lot of price lists. At its yearly Connect event on 28 November, Somnium announced Dreamport, a launcher inside its social VR platform for finding, downloading and starting community VR mods of flat games. It launches on 17 December and you must own the underlying game. Flat2VR Studios is the first partner with five paid titles: Roboquest VR and I Am Your Beast VR in December, and three more in the first quarter of 2027.

Why it matters VR mods are scattered across forums and GitHub; a storefront with an SDK (C# scripting) and multiplayer could make them teachable examples. Road to VR saw only the live presentation, with no hands-on. The platform is adults-only and requires real-ID registration.

Regulation · Report

A Dutch court will rule by 24 December on halting Ray-Ban Meta sales

On Thursday the Amsterdam District Court heard the consumer association Consumentenbond, Offlimits and The Firewall ask for sales of Meta's Ray-Ban glasses to stop in the Netherlands until privacy protections improve, and for an end to using captured footage to train AI. Meta argued that the recording light warns bystanders and that a maker cannot answer for all misuse; EssilorLuxottica called a ban disproportionate. Penalties of €100,000 a day, capped at €100 million, are reported second-hand. In the same week, per VR.org, the UK government told petitioners a blanket ban would not be proportionate, and Germany's consumer federation sued the glasses' maker in Berlin.

Why it matters This is the first European court asked to pull camera glasses from sale, and the answer lands before Christmas. I read TechTimes (which cites Reuters) and VR.org, not the court filings.

Developers

Meta's VR Start contest drew 2,105 entries, a third of them built for the web

VR.org counted the public gallery of Meta's second VR Start Developer Competition, which closed on 18 November: 2,105 projects from 7,247 registered participants, up from 655 projects last year. The prize pool fell from $1.5 million to $1 million across 20 awards. Unity is named on 51.5% of entries (68.1% last year) and a web stack such as WebXR or Meta's Immersive Web SDK on 34.6% (11.6%). An AI coding agent is named on 355 entries, and 84% list a single creator. Winners are due around 11 December.

Why it matters It is a rough census of how newcomers build for Quest now: more solo, more web, more agent-written. The tool data is a voluntary free-text field compared with last year's form field, a mismatch VR.org flags itself. Meta has not confirmed the count.

Geology · Coasts

Africa's coastal megacities are sinking as the sea rises

A Virginia Tech team led by Oluwaseyi Dasho, with Manoochehr Shirzaei, mapped vertical land motion in 20 African coastal cities from thousands of Sentinel-1 satellite radar observations, then combined it with water levels, elevation, population and building footprints. Alexandria is subsiding at a median of about 6 mm a year and Lagos at about 5 mm. Across the cities, more than 7 million people, 1 million buildings and roughly $180 billion in assets are on track to be at flood risk by 2050. In Alexandria, subsidence raises projected relative sea-level rise by about 36% by 2050.

Why it matters Global sea-level projections carry coarse land-motion estimates; measured sinking widened Alexandria's flood-exposed area by about 15% over the IPCC's figures. The drivers named are groundwater pumping, sediment compaction and construction. Read from the press release; the projections depend on the study's scenario choices.

Ecology · Climate

Leatherbacks are nesting in Ecuador, 1,000 miles from their usual beaches

A review of more than 45 years of sightings, nests and strandings on Ecuador's coast, with Simon Fraser University's Juan José Alava as lead co-author, reports record eastern Pacific leatherback nesting there in the 2025–26 season. Nesting in Ecuador rose during cold La Niña episodes over the past decade, and egg counts and hatch rates matched the traditional grounds in Mexico and Costa Rica. One tracked female laid 146 eggs over the season, of which 134 hatched.

Why it matters This population has fallen by more than 90% since the 1980s, so new nesting range is rare good news. The release gives no total nest count, and "may be adapting" is the authors' own hedge. ScienceDaily picked the story up on 27 November; the release is from 9 November.

Carbon · Update

That "65% more CO2 without marine life" figure comes from a policy brief

Yesterday's brief had only a headline. The release behind it describes a policy brief for the COP31 climate talks, produced by the European OceanICU project with Patrizia Ziveri of the Autonomous University of Barcelona among the contributors, and no journal paper. It states that without organisms carrying carbon to the deep ocean, atmospheric CO2 in 2100 would be 35% to 65% higher than projected. It also says the Earth system models used by the IPCC leave out fish, marine mammals and daily vertical migrations.

Why it matters The brief urges caution on marine carbon removal, counting only storage that lasts more than 100 years, and says restoring reefs and mussel beds does not add carbon capture. The 35–65% range is a modelled what-if with no method given in the release.

VRST '26 · Collaboration

Steering a tabletop avatar's gaze: immersive VR beats two mixed-reality options

arXiv has posted no new listing since Thursday, so today's paper is from this month's VRST in Sendai. Mosquera, Elsayed, Kruijff and colleagues (Siemens and Graz University of Technology) studied a remote expert who places an avatar inside a tabletop miniature of a distant site using a tangible prop. They compared three ways to direct the avatar's gaze: switching to full VR and turning your head, a floating screen with head control, and an on-avatar screen with a touchpad. With 29 participants on two fault-finding tasks, the VR condition gave the best overall performance and experience.

Why it matters The world-in-miniature pattern is common in remote assistance, and this suggests that dropping fully into the avatar's view beats staying in mixed reality. Participants were company employees (18 men, 11 women). The abstract gives no effect sizes, and the results section was not read in full.
