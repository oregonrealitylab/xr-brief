Meta's phone app pushes Worlds aside, and may become "Meta VR"

UploadVR reports that a new design of the Meta Horizon mobile app is reaching some users. The Worlds-centred feed is gone and the VR store is the landing page. Worlds now sits low in search results or in its own search tab. This follows the split Meta announced at Connect, with Worlds moving mostly to a separate Horizon Create app.

Datamined, not announced: strings in a November build point to the name "Meta VR", and the same build holds setup videos for the Meta VR Glasses showing a puck to charge, app pairing, and prescription inserts in a Zenni-branded case.

Why it matters The companion app is where most Quest owners browse and buy. Making it a store first is the clearest sign yet that Meta now treats VR and Worlds as two products.

Why it matters Meta says thousands of titles and international sales arrive with its $1,299.99 VR Glasses in spring 2027. This catalogue is being built for that device.

Nutrients · Science

A quarter of the air's nitrogen gift to the ocean is organic

A team co-led from HKUST and SUSTech measured organic and inorganic nitrogen directly in 418 marine aerosol samples from 21 cruises, from the tropics to both poles. Fed into a global atmospheric chemistry model, the data give 12.8 million tonnes of organic nitrogen deposited to the oceans each year, about 27% of all atmospheric nitrogen deposition. In nitrogen-poor waters such as the central Atlantic the share is about half.

Roughly 70% comes from land, with biomass burning the largest single source at 4.8 million tonnes.

Why it matters Wildfire smoke becomes an ocean fertiliser term that models mostly lack. The total is a model estimate with no uncertainty range in the report I read, and the effect on productivity is suggested, not measured.

Ocean tech

WHOI and JPL unveil a 250 kg robot rated for the deepest trenches

Deep Venture is a modular autonomous vehicle rated to 11,000 m. It does seafloor photogrammetry, carries chemical and biological samplers, and can land on the bottom. In June it dived to about 6,000 m near Bermuda and scouted sites for the crewed sub Alvin; in 2024 it made 13 dives to 5,000 m in the Gulf of Alaska.

Why it matters Full-depth access without a large ship is the gap in hadal science. The deepest reported dive is still well short of the rating, and the announcement gives no cost or schedule.

Deep sea · Mining

Metals shaken from nodule sediment damage mussels within four days

Researchers at Ocean University of China agitated nodules and sediment from 5,365 m in the Clarion-Clipperton Zone in seawater, which released metals at 1.8 to 11.6 times control levels. Thick-shell mussels exposed for 96 hours showed stress responses in the gills within a day and, in the digestive gland, a lipid-damage marker up to 6.1 times the control level.

Why it matters It arrives two days after the first US Pacific seabed lease sale. The limits are large: a coastal species, at surface temperature and pressure, for four days. The report gives no sample sizes.

Ecology · Science

Culling seals and sea lions would do little for West Coast salmon

An ecosystem model of the waters off northern California, Oregon and Washington, led by Dylan Gomes, simulated cutting back seals, sea lions, seabirds and other predators. Chinook and coho gained little, because the same predators eat fish that compete with or prey on young salmon. Reducing Pacific hake produced the largest modelled gain, though more hake fishing would raise salmon bycatch.

Why it matters Culls are a live policy request in the Pacific Northwest. The authors allow that removing individual animals at river bottlenecks can help. The news report gives no effect sizes.

Deep sea · Microbes

One tiny-genomed bacterium dominates the skin of sea pigs

A University of Tokyo-led team sequenced tissues from eight deep-sea sea pigs of three species. A single new bacterium, Candidatus Abyssoplasma scotoplanesicola, made up more than 70% of bacterial marker-gene sequences in the body wall. Its genome is 649,000 bases, about a seventh of E. coli's, and over a quarter of it codes for surface proteins, some more than 5,000 amino acids long.

Why it matters Whether the microbe helps or merely lodges is unknown, and eight animals is a small sample.

arXiv · XR + AI

Reading AI-written code in a headset that summarises what you have seen

A quiet weekend for new work, and arXiv again blocked most abstract pages, so this section is short. Zakharov and colleagues first interviewed 13 experts in AI and XR. They then built two XR code-reading prototypes. In one, code fades once you have looked at it (4 developers tried it). In the other, viewed sections are replaced by short descriptions, with head direction deciding when a swap is offered and manual input to accept or undo it (8 developers).

Participants said descriptions gave a useful overview of unfamiliar code. They also said comparing a description with its source was hard, and that controls unlike a normal editor added effort.

Why it matters These are self-reports from very small groups, submitted to an IUI 2027 workshop. The design rule is still worth borrowing: keep the original one glance away from any AI summary.

arXiv · Survey

A map of 163 smart-glasses papers, in four layers

Gao and colleagues survey smart-glasses research from 2020 to October 2026. They sort 163 papers into research resources, sensing and perception, interaction and assistance, and applications. They also catalogue 14 hardware platforms and 10 datasets, and treat energy, latency, privacy and social acceptability as constraints across every layer.

Why it matters A ready reading map for a seminar in the term when Android XR glasses reach shops. Abstract only; I have not checked how papers were selected.
