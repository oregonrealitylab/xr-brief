Meta's $1M hands-only competition closes; winners around 11 December

A quiet day: the trade sites published almost nothing over the weekend, and Black Friday deals fill the feeds. Submissions to Meta's second Start developer competition closed on 18 November. It offers $1M as 20 awards of $20,000 to $100,000 across entertainment, gaming and productivity. Entries must work with hands alone from start to finish, ideally seated, and satisfy within about ten minutes.

In today's weekly roundup, Skarredghost, whose team entered, says many strong demos have appeared on social media. He also finds many of them casual mini-games, and the tooling unchanged from a year ago. That is one participant's opinion.

Why it matters The brief describes the content Meta wants for its VR Glasses: controller-free, seated and short. The winners list will show how far hand tracking alone can carry an app.

The PC VR mod debate gets a rebuttal

Friday's Road to VR opinion piece argued that flatscreen-to-VR mods serve existing owners without growing the market. VR.org answered on Saturday: PC gaming's biggest titles began as mods, and on 21 November Counter-Strike 2 had about 1.06 million concurrent players and Dota 2 about 753,000. The author adds that Half-Life 2: VR Mod holds 97% positive from 5,583 English reviews. He locates the real risk in publishers' lawyers, citing Take-Two's 2022 takedown of Luke Ross's mods.

Why it matters Read together, the two pieces make a seminar on what sustains a platform's content. Both are opinion, and the figures are the authors' own.

PullFit pairs hand tracking with resistance bands

PullFit, a $9.99 Early Access fitness app for Quest by solo developer James Hough, uses hand tracking only, with resistance bands as the equipment. A 2D stick-figure mirror or a 3D holographic partner demonstrates the form. Two people can train together in one room or online, and up to eight can pass the headset around. UploadVR's reviewer used it for six weeks on a complimentary key.

Why it matters A cheap physical prop supplies the resistance that hand tracking cannot. That pattern suits a student project.

Oxygen · Paleo

The North Pacific's low-oxygen zone followed the Atlantic's lead

A reconstruction published today uses 2,569 counts of seafloor foraminifera from 11 sediment cores across the subarctic and eastern North Pacific. Over 55,000 years the oxygen minimum zone expanded during abrupt northern warm events, roughly doubling from about 3,000 km to at least 6,000 km. It shrank to 1,000 to 2,000 km when Atlantic overturning weakened. The Pacific lagged by a few centuries at most, and an Earth-system model reproduces the link.

Why it matters Yesterday's item, from nitrogen isotopes in one core at the zone's southern margin, found almost no glacial change. The two studies use different proxies, places and timescales, so they need not conflict, but the question is open. Oxygen estimates here carry about ±45% error, and the model forcing is idealised.

Geology

Mediterranean dead-water layers tied to Atlantic rain as well as the Nile

Sapropels are dark, organic-rich layers laid down when the deep Mediterranean lost its oxygen. The usual explanation is monsoon-fed Nile floods. A sediment-chemistry proxy for winter rainfall at an ocean drilling site off south-west Iberia, compared with western Mediterranean surface salinity over 350,000 years, shows low-salinity events and sapropels coinciding with wetter Iberian winters. The authors conclude that moisture carried in from the Atlantic also drove the basin's salinity and deep ventilation.

Why it matters It amends a textbook mechanism. The Atlantic signal rests on one core and an indirect proxy, and the proposed link to a North Atlantic Oscillation-like pattern is tentative. I read the abstract and methods preview only.

Climate · Coasts

Later freeze-up means more extreme waves at Canada's northernmost community

Researchers from Université du Québec à Rimouski, with co-authors from Ausuittuq (Grise Fiord), Nunavut, split extreme water levels there into tide, surge and wave runup. Runup supplies about 30% of annual maximum water levels and directly drives a quarter of extreme events. With CMIP6 wind and sea-ice projections and a locally calibrated wave model, extreme wave events become 41% more frequent by mid-century under a middle emissions scenario and 62% under a high one, relative to 1985 to 2000.

Why it matters It puts a local number on an Arctic hazard usually described in general terms. One site, a simplified wave model, and an early-release paper whose abstract gives no uncertainty ranges.

Communications Earth & Environment
Corals · Climate

Deep reefs offer little shelter from marine heatwaves

This one is from 10 November. It fills a gap on a quiet day: NOC, Scripps and MBARI posted nothing new this weekend. Simulated daily temperatures for 1993 to 2023, down to 150 m, show heatwaves covering more than half of tropical reef area in 8 of the 13 years since 2010. At 90 to 150 m they lasted 15 days or more in all but two years, at about 2 °C above normal.

Why it matters The idea that deep reefs can reseed damaged shallow ones assumes the deep stays cool. These are modelled temperatures, not instrument records, and the study includes no bleaching observations.

arXiv · Social XR

Users keep believing whoever was announced first

Zhang and Steed (UCL) asked whether people notice when a virtual human passes between an AI agent and a human-driven avatar mid-conversation. In a within-subject VR study, 24 participants interviewed a virtual human under four conditions, then rated who was in control, moment by moment, while reviewing recordings. Perceived agency followed the controller disclosed at the start and did not clearly update after a handover. Co-presence and likeability were higher when the human took over later.

Why it matters If first impressions anchor, a single disclosure at the start of a hybrid session will mislead. The sample is small, the interview had five scripted questions, and the agent's replies were pre-recorded, not a live language model.

arXiv · Accessibility

AdaptType fits VR text entry to each user's abilities

Kong and colleagues, with Jacob Wobbrock, built a multimodal system for people with upper-body motor impairments. Users configure input techniques from ability-based recommendations and fix errors by voice. With 10 participants, customised techniques beat gaze-only and hand-only baselines. Voice correction cut the failure rate from 15.1% to 2.0%, and the abstract reports 36.26 words per minute at a 0.36% error rate.

Why it matters Ability-based design has rarely been applied to VR typing. Ten participants, and the abstract does not say which configuration produced the headline speed.

arXiv · Safety

A thickening fog in place of the grid boundary

Degenhard and colleagues at Ulm replaced the play-area grid with particles that thicken near the edge. In a within-subjects study with 11 people, reaction distance matched Meta's Guardian grid (0.15 m against 0.14 m). Presence was higher, and seven preferred the particles. The Unity code is open source, and the paper is headed for MUM '26 in Glasgow next week.

Why it matters It is a boundary cue that belongs to the scene and can be reused. The authors acknowledge a confound: the particles appeared earlier and stayed visible about three times as long as the grid.
