SteamVR beta brings chroma-key passthrough to Quest over Steam Link

A quiet weekend: nothing new was published today, and most of what follows is from 19 to 21 November. The SteamVR 2.19.4 beta adds chroma-key support for Room View when streaming to a Quest. Wherever a game draws the key colour, the headset's camera feed shows through. The Steam Frame got the same option on 13 November.

The settings offer two modes, "Key Content" and "Protect Content". VR.org, which read Valve's notes but has not tested the build, says Valve does not explain the second mode, name the key colour, or list supported Quest models.

Why it matters Chroma key is a cheap route to mixed reality for PC VR titles, since a developer only has to paint a colour. Valve is also keeping Quest close to parity with its own headset.

Pico's $50,000 contest pays double for native Android apps built with AI agents

The PICO Space Program "Build with AI" competition offers $50,000 across 32 cash prizes for spatial apps for PICO OS 6 written with AI coding agents. The native Android/Kotlin track pays $10,000 and $5,000 at the top, against $5,000 and $2,000 for Unity. Registration closes 1 February 2027, and entrants can work in the emulator without a headset.

VR.org read the rules and reports that XR Bootcamp GmbH is the sole sponsor and administrator, with Pico as brand and hardware partner. Report, not confirmed by Pico: the same article says the setup documents refer to a "PICO Space Pro (Project Swan)" slated for 2 December.

Why it matters The prize weighting shows where Pico wants developers to go, which is away from game engines. It is also a ready assignment brief for an XR development course.

Climate · Science

Antarctic ice cliffs collapse in bursts, then stall

Also quiet here: nothing dated this weekend, so these are from 17 to 19 November. An MIT, WHOI and Michigan team used ICESat-2 laser altimetry to follow more than 15,000 Antarctic ice cliffs every three months from 2019 to 2023. The average cliff stood about 30 m, and only a few passed the roughly 100 m height at which theory says cliffs fail under their own weight. The tallest was 127 m.

At the four tallest sites, cliffs sometimes retreated faster than models predict, but briefly, and some glaciers advanced again. Thick ice mélange, deep water and rising bedrock acted as brakes.

Why it matters Runaway cliff collapse drives the worst-case sea-level projections, and models trigger it on height alone. Four years and four detailed sites is a short record, and the authors do not call the ice sheet safe.

Oxygen · Paleo

A Pacific low-oxygen zone barely moved through the ice ages

Nitrogen isotopes locked inside foraminifera shells from a core at 6°N in the eastern Pacific span about 265,000 years. Values at the last glacial maximum (10.3‰, 14 samples) nearly match the late Holocene (10.8‰, 13 samples). The southern edge of the North Pacific oxygen-deficient zone was therefore about the same in cold and warm periods. Its variation followed orbital sunlight patterns, not global temperature.

Why it matters Older bulk-sediment records suggested these zones shrank in glacial times; the authors attribute that signal to alteration of the sediment. This is one core at the zone's margin, so forecasts for a warmer ocean remain inference.

El Niño · Observatories

Monterey Bay's diatoms vanish as deep water off British Columbia sets a heat record

MBARI's imaging instrument saw diatoms drop on 4 October and largely disappear by 8 October. Satellite chlorophyll off Big Sur fell about tenfold in places, and a mooring logged a record 19.22 °C at 60 m on 12 November in a series nearly 40 years long. Further north, Ocean Networks Canada measured 8.21 °C at 400 m in Barkley Canyon on 8 November, 2.49 °C above the site's long-term mean.

Why it matters Cabled observatories are recording a strong El Niño below the surface as it happens. Neither report is peer reviewed, both regions were already warm this year, and the reported wildlife effects are anecdotal so far.

Ecology · Fisheries

Young emperor penguins spend their first year where krill boats fish

Satellite tags on 48 juveniles from three Weddell Sea colonies showed a consistent trip of more than 1,500 km north to the Polar Front and back with the sea ice. Depending on the colony, the birds spent 15% to 32% of the year inside proposed marine protected areas. Juveniles from Jason Peninsula spent over 60% of February and March in recently fished zones.

Why it matters The Weddell Sea protected area is stalled at CCAMLR, and this gives it a map of where juveniles go. The study shows overlap with fishing, not harm from it.

Ocean tech

MBARI runs six long-range robots to rehearse offshore wind monitoring

In two trials between August and mid-October, MBARI's long-range autonomous vehicles logged about 2,000 hours and more than 4,000 km in Monterey Bay. They collected over 100 environmental DNA samples along with plankton imaging and acoustics.

Why it matters Docked, persistent fleets could replace ship surveys for environmental baselines at deep-water wind leases. This was a demonstration, with no published results or cost comparison yet.

In brief

  • Heatwaves After the 2021 Pacific Northwest heat dome, large mussels died high on the shore and small ones died low on it. A heat-budget model credits evaporative cooling (Proceedings B). One site, and the release gives no counts. University of Washington release
  • Corals Heated to 32 °C, a sensitive Acropora lost 74% of its symbionts and a tolerant Pavona 15%, though both hosted the same symbiont types. The tolerant host kept its membrane lipids stable. Three colonies per species. Science China Press release
  • Fossils A 2.7 m pygmy sperm whale relative from Peru, about 6 million years old, had both nostrils shifted left and a snout angled 31° down. The authors read it as a seafloor feeder, from one specimen. ScienceDaily

VRST 2026 names its best papers

The fresh arXiv listings held almost nothing new for XR, so awards lead. Two best papers: "SpatialSlides", which integrates presentation software with XR for 3D exploration (Nam, Chheang, Miao), and "Your Place or Mine?", on how sharing environmental context affects social presence in remote XR (Kaiser and colleagues).

Honorable mentions went to work on inferring competing task demands for pedestrians using AR, real versus simulated exertion in VR cycling, and engagement as the mediator of VR pain relief.

Why it matters Titles and authors only; I found no abstracts or preprints, so I cannot report methods or results. The social presence paper is the one to find when the proceedings open.

arXiv · Cybersickness

Three sliders report cybersickness by symptom without adding load

Saha and colleagues asked whether separate in-headset sliders for nausea, oculomotor discomfort and disorientation tell you more than one discomfort slider. In a between-subjects study with 52 people on a VR rollercoaster, each symptom slider correlated best with its matching questionnaire subscale, while the single slider correlated with all of them. Usability, workload and attention on a concurrent task did not differ significantly.

Why it matters Continuous, symptom-level labels are what prediction models lack, and the dataset is public. One stimulus, and the abstract gives no effect sizes.

arXiv · Spatial audio

Virtual sound pulls distance judgments closer

Arend and colleagues ran two experiments with 45 participants in total, using real-time room acoustics and a virtual copy of a large lab. Binaural rendering was judged plausible in both audio AR and VR. In a walking distance task, any virtualisation lowered accuracy and raised effort, though performance stayed high. Virtual audio biased judgments toward nearer distances.

Why it matters Plausible is not the same as accurate, which matters for training and navigation tasks. Abstract only, with no effect sizes.
