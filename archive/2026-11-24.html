Meta's hands-only contest: 2,105 entries, a third built on web tech

Thanksgiving week is quiet: no hardware, funding or platform announcements, so this follow-up leads. VR.org counted the public Devpost gallery for Meta's Start competition, which closed on 18 November, and found 2,105 projects from 7,247 registrants, against 655 and 2,860 last year. From self-listed "Built With" tags, Unity's share fell from 68.1% to 51.5%, web technology (WebXR, Immersive Web SDK, three.js) rose from 11.6% to 34.6%, and Unreal fell to 3.8%. About 84% of entries name a single creator, and roughly one in six names an AI coding assistant.

Why it matters It is rare numerical evidence of what XR developers build with, and it bears on what to teach. Caveats: one outlet's scrape of free-text tags, compared against a different form field in 2025, and Meta has published no official count.

A VR mod for the new Ocarina of Time remake

HookshotVR, by a modder called SqueezyLemonade, forks an open-source PC recompilation of the Switch 2 remake and adds first-person view, tracked controllers, a wrist display and a launcher. It uses OpenXR, is at version 1.0.2 on GitHub, and needs your own copy of the game. Road to VR reports good results on Linux with an RTX 4070 and a Quest 3; the article calls it technically still in alpha.

Why it matters It extends last week's mod thread: recompilation plus OpenXR put a current console exclusive into VR within months of release. The legal exposure is my inference, not the article's.

SteamVR beta brings chroma-key passthrough to Quest

SteamVR 2.19.4 beta, posted by Valve on 19 November, adds chroma key to Room View for Quest headsets streaming over Steam Link: wherever a game draws the key colour, the headset shows its camera view. Marine science 4 items + briefs · 5 min

Air–sea · Climate

The sea trades at least 73 organic gases with the air

A University of Copenhagen-led team measured fluxes of volatile organic compounds by eddy covariance at Utö island in the Baltic for 51 days in summer 2024. They tracked 73 compounds, 48 of them identified, which the release puts at roughly ten times more than most earlier studies. Dimethyl sulphide emission peaked in late July and fell to near zero by late August as cyanobacteria declined. Acetone and acetaldehyde were steadily absorbed by the sea, and acetic acid was emitted.

Why it matters Climate models represent marine trace gases with a few compounds and little seasonality. This is one site over one season, with 50 to 100% uncertainty on the unidentified compounds.

Carbon

A global map of where marine carbon removal works best

Published today, this modelling study puts three approaches in one framework: adding alkalinity, extracting dissolved carbon directly, and combined additions such as the ocean end of enhanced rock weathering. One-month pulses were released in 690 ocean regions in four seasons and followed for 15 years. Direct removal approaches full efficiency in most subpolar and equatorial regions but falls short in the subtropics and high latitudes. For combined additions, 3 to 20% of the stored CO2 has leaked back by year 15, more than an earlier estimate of about 10%.

Why it matters Verification schemes need a common yardstick across methods and places. It is one coarse model with fixed atmospheric CO2, no coastal detail, and no accounting for upstream emissions.

Deep sea

A drowned coral atoll on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge

Schmidt Ocean Institute reports that a 35-day Brazilian-led expedition on R/V Falkor (too) surveyed the 950 km Romanche Fracture Zone. It found a sunken atoll about 300 m tall, its top 900 m down, with living corals on it. The release dates it to about 5 million years and says it confirms a 30-year-old hypothesis that atolls once stood in the mid-Atlantic.

Why it matters It is the first visual survey of one of the Atlantic's largest fracture zones, and a candidate for high-seas protection. This is a press release, not a paper: no dating method is given, and the new species are suspected, not confirmed.

Nutrients

Organic nitrogen makes up a quarter of what the air feeds the ocean

A Science paper from 20 November, led from Hong Kong and Shenzhen, analysed 418 marine aerosol samples from 21 cruises and combined them with a global atmospheric chemistry model. It puts organic nitrogen deposition to the oceans at 12.8 million tonnes a year, about 27% of all nitrogen deposited. Biomass burning is the largest source, and fires and human activity together supply about 70%.

Why it matters Most ocean biogeochemistry models count only inorganic nitrogen. I read the release only; the global total is model-derived, and the release does not say how much of this nitrogen organisms can use.

Awards · ISS

Best paper: a robot swarm as a fluid extension of the hand

ISS 2026 in Turin has posted its awards. Best paper goes to Fluidic Body (Eguchi and colleagues, Tsukuba and OMRON SINIC X), presented today. Each robot is a particle in a fluid simulation: an open hand makes the swarm flow around obstacles, and a closed hand makes it hold together to push objects. In a VR study with 17 people, all completed a combined avoid-and-transport task, against 8 with a standard collision-avoidance controller. Workload roughly halved and sense of agency rose.

Why it matters A clear mapping for controlling many bodies with one hand. Simpler single tasks showed no reliable difference, and the fixed-parameter baseline may flatter the result.

Accessibility · ISS

For autistic adults, how a cue starts matters more than how many there are

An ISS honourable mention, presented remotely tomorrow. Meng, Chen and colleagues had 20 autistic adults in China try five versions of the same science content on Quest 3, from plain text to gaze-triggered light and sound with forearm vibration and warmth. Abrupt, click-triggered spatial audio scored lowest (2.97 of 5). Gradual gaze-triggered cues scored 4.05 and the full multisensory version 4.43, though its lead over plain text was not statistically significant.

Why it matters It questions the habit of equating "sensory-friendly" with "less". Each condition bundled several changes at once, so no single factor is isolated.

Tools · ISS

AdapTest lets designers test adaptive interfaces inside the headset

Rhee and colleagues (KAIST, with Adobe Research and NUS) pair a web editor with a visionOS app. Designers write if-then rules over viewing distance, brightness and occlusion, then try them live. Against Figma with static previews, 12 designers covered 2.4 of 3 environmental factors, up from 1.4, and tested about five times as often. Workload and usability scores did not differ, and the two tools were given different tasks and time limits.

arXiv · Telepresence

Steering a robot's camera with your head beats a joystick

Zaman, Veas and colleagues ran a two-by-two study with 17 operators guiding a quadruped robot through a physical maze from a headset. Head-guided camera control went with faster completion, fewer wall contacts and lower workload. An omnidirectional treadmill improved the experience but not performance. Abstract only, and it gives no effect sizes.
